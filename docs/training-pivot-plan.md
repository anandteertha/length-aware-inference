# Proposed next phase: train a compact student and a length-aware escalation gate

**Status:** research plan, not a frozen experiment or a claim of novelty or improvement. The author's course rubric and presentation deadline are still needed to set the final scope. This plan adds a training-based phase to the completed [inference-only project](project-report.md); it preserves all earlier negative and positive observations.

## Why change direction

The [original proposal](../arrao6_project_proposal.pdf) explicitly said no training was needed. Its bounded fixed-shape selector was implemented and evaluated: the broad workload favored dynamic-only ONNX, and the later [break-even map](breakeven-map-results.md) found no fixed-route candidate across eleven real-text lengths. Testing more bucket sizes or editing text lengths until one wins would screen many conditions without creating a new learned method. It could also change sentiment labels or produce an accidental positive result. The next question should shift from *which input shape is fastest?* to **which inputs actually need the full model's computation?**

## Recommended research question

Can a **trained 3-layer DistilBERT student** classify easy sentiment sentences, while a **learned, length-and-confidence-aware gate** sends only cases likely to benefit to the existing 6-layer teacher, thereby improving the measured CPU accuracy–latency trade-off?

The teacher is the pinned SST-2 classifier already used in the repository. The student will copy its embeddings, first three transformer layers, and classifier initialization, then update student weights on labeled SST-2 examples. Its training objective will combine ground-truth cross-entropy with teacher-logit distillation:

`loss = (1 − α) · CE(student, label) + α · T² · KL(teacher_T || student_T)`.

`α` and temperature `T` are chosen on development data only. The teacher is frozen. We will compare an **untrained truncation**, a **supervised-only trained student**, and a **uniformly distilled student**. A prespecified fourth arm, if the course deadline permits, uses **CPU-cost-weighted distillation**: multiply each example's KL term by its measured full-model latency at that token length, divided by median latency and clipped to `[0.5, 2]`. Measure that cost on training-only inputs and keep the CE term unchanged. Train the weighted and uniform students with the same seed and budget. This tests whether teaching the student more strongly on expensive fallback cases improves the final accuracy-latency curve. It is an experiment-specific method hypothesis, not a claim of first invention; cascade-aware training is prior work. All trained arms include real gradient updates. The trained student alone is useful even if the gate does not work.

The cascade first runs the student. The gate sees its confidence/margin and original token length, then either accepts the student or runs the full teacher. For gate training, the target is the teacher's *incremental correctness* over the student: teacher helps (+1), both have the same correctness (0), or teacher harms (−1). A lightweight gate estimates those outcomes; measured teacher CPU cost as a function of length enters the routing decision. This is a testable project contribution, **not a claim that model cascades, distillation, early exits, or cost-aware routing were invented here**. We will compare it with global confidence thresholds and length-only routing to test whether the joint information helps.

The available laptop has a 6-layer, 66.96-million-parameter DistilBERT teacher, ~31.7 GiB RAM, and no CUDA GPU. A [repeatable feasibility probe](../scripts/training_feasibility.py) copied the first three layers into a 45.69-million-parameter student, used eight real training sentences padded to 21 tokens, and completed five measured CPU optimizer steps at four threads. The [archived run](../results/training-feasibility.json) took 363-721 ms per step (median 527 ms); the classifier weights changed, and the highest observed process RSS was 1,477 MiB with both models and the optimizer resident. An earlier run had a 393 ms median, so these tiny pilots show runtime variation. They establish that small-batch gradient training works on this laptop. They do **not** establish an accuracy gain, an epoch runtime, or inference memory. The student and teacher must be benchmarked as actual resident models. The cascade may lose if too many requests rerun the teacher; FLOP or layer counts alone cannot establish a speedup.

## Data and evaluation separation

1. Pin dataset and model revisions. Partition the SST-2 **training** split by original row ID into student fitting, student development, gate fitting, and gate calibration sets before training. Never tune weights, gate thresholds, or loss settings on GLUE validation.
2. The released teacher was already trained on SST-2 training data. Training-derived gate results therefore do not establish classifier generalization; final accuracy must be measured on a separate labeled split. Use GLUE SST-2 validation (872 rows) for the new method's final in-domain comparison, disclosing that this repository previously reported aggregate results on it. Add an independent sentiment test split if time and rubric allow, with source-overlap and domain limitations recorded. The [Rotten Tomatoes dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/cornell-movie-review-data/rotten_tomatoes) lists a 1,066-row test split as one candidate; inspect overlap before calling it independent.
3. Record exact train/development/calibration/test IDs and hashes. Do not truncate inputs silently. Freeze the student checkpoint, gate, thresholds, metrics, and comparisons before final evaluation. Keep every failed or unfavorable run.
4. Examine performance across prespecified token-length strata drawn from real examples, merging sparse strata when needed. Report example counts and paired student/teacher errors in each stratum. More artificial shortening or padding can test mechanisms, but those modified texts do not substitute for natural-text accuracy evidence.

## Experiment ladder and decision gates

| Stage | Work | Evidence to produce | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Feasibility | Time one student forward/backward step, export and check a three-layer model, estimate resident memory. | Reproducible smoke report and numerical checks. | If training is impractical on this CPU, switch to trained intermediate exit heads on a frozen backbone and label the change before evaluation. |
| B. Student training | Train supervised-only, uniform-distillation, and, if time permits, cost-weighted-distillation students with the same data, seed, budget, and checkpoint rule. | Loss curves, development accuracy, agreement with teacher, training time, model size, ablation of cost weighting. | Select training settings on student development data, not final validation. |
| C. Routing | Fit a small benefit gate; compare student-only, full teacher, length-only, global confidence threshold, and length-plus-confidence cost-aware gate. | Exit/fallback rates, errors corrected and introduced, gate calibration, accuracy by length. | Choose a single operating point on gate calibration data under a stated accuracy budget. |
| D. Deployment test | Export student to ONNX, run each method in matched randomized CPU processes with all required models resident. | End-to-end and inference-only median/p95, accuracy, peak RSS, model size, startup, Pareto plot. | Claim an efficiency improvement only if the final held-out comparison beats the **strong dynamic ONNX teacher**, not merely a slower PyTorch baseline. |
| E. Presentation | Explain the failed static-shape hypothesis, learned alternative, training ablations, routing mechanism, and final trade-off. | Reproducible report and figures with exact denominators and limitations. | Present a supported result, including a negative outcome if the learned cascade does not win. |

The primary presentation figure should be an **accuracy versus measured CPU latency Pareto plot**, with model size and memory alongside it. Additional figures: student training curves; fallback fraction by token length; a confusion/error-correction breakdown; and a latency decomposition showing the cost of student-first plus teacher fallback. Report mean, median, and p95 latency: early routing can improve average work while leaving p95 unchanged when more than 5% of requests use the full path. The original dynamic ONNX result remains the deployment baseline.

If schedule permits, add a separately labeled INT8 dynamic-quantized teacher and student comparison. Quantization is a standard efficiency baseline, not the claimed new method; it may set a stronger bar for the cascade on this CPU. Fix quantization settings and numerical tolerances before final comparison.

The success target to freeze before final evaluation is: no more than **one percentage point** loss versus the full teacher's accuracy and at least **15% lower measured median end-to-end CPU latency**, with peak resident RSS under the existing 1,536 MiB cap. These are targets, not predicted results. Report the full curve even if no point meets them. Confidence intervals or paired uncertainty summaries should use independent examples/process blocks as appropriate; repeated requests in one process are not independent samples.

## What is original here, and what is prior work

[DeeBERT](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.204/) and [FastBERT](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.537/) already train intermediate classifiers and use adaptive inference. [CascadeBERT](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.43/) and [model cascading](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.756/) already study calibrated cascades and cost/accuracy trade-offs. [Cascade-aware training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00060) shows that the student itself can be optimized for a cascade. Our course-level contribution would be the **measured edge-CPU study and ablation of a trained, length-conditioned teacher-benefit gate over a genuinely trained compact student**, rooted in the earlier failure of shape-only routing. A literature review and the course rubric are needed before calling that algorithm globally novel.

If the learned cascade pays too much for recomputing the teacher, a **shared multi-exit model** is the next defined alternative: train exit heads after layers 2 and 4 and reuse intermediate computation. That is also established prior art. We should only pursue it as a separately frozen second experiment, not as an unreported switch after seeing the final test.
